# Model Tunning & Optimization

## Introduction
Model Tuning is the architectural refinement of a Machine Learning system. It is the process of moving beyond default settings to find the "Sweet Spot" between a model that is too simple (**Underfitting**) and one that is too complex (**Overfitting**). 

Instead of just "running a model," Tuning is an engineering workflow used to maximize specific performance metrics—such as **Recall** for safety-critical tasks—by optimizing how the model sees the data and how its internal mathematical engine operates.

---


## 1. Splits Methods (Validation Strategies)
Instead of a single "final exam" (simple Train/Test), you perform several "simulated tests" by rotating the data. This ensures the model actually learned the patterns rather than just memorizing the answers (overfitting).

When you do a simple split, you run the risk of "bad luck." For example, split can put all hard cases on the test and easy ones on the workout. This generates lying metrics.

> Here our focus it's to see if our model and the metrics are True or simple a hidden lie

* **Why use it?**
1. **Robustness:** Ensures performance wasn't just "luck" from a single favorable data split.
2. **Stability Measurement:** Allows you to calculate the Standard Deviation; if accuracy varies wildly between splits, the model is unstable.

**Types**
1. **Cross Validation:** Divides the dataset into $K$ fixed blocks (folds) and train. Each block serves as the test set once, while the others are used for training.
    * **Use:** `cross_val_score()` 
    * **Metrics (list):** scores = `cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=z)`
        - Here the cross_val_score will return a list with all the splits and their accuracy
    * **Mean:** `print(scores.mean())`


> K-FOLD AND STRATIFIED ONLY CRIATES THE FOLD, WHILE THE CROSS_VAL TRAIN AND TEST!!!!!!!!!

2. **K-Fold:** Divides the dataset into $K$ fixed blocks (folds). Each block serves as the test set once, while the others are used for training **DO NOT TRAIN**.
    * **Rule:** Every single data point is tested exactly once.
    * **Best for:** Small to medium-sized datasets. | `KFold()` |

3. **Stratified (K-Fold Classification Version):** A "safety lock" applied to K-Fold or Shuffle **DO NOT TRAIN**.
    * **What it does:** Ensures each split maintains the same class proportions (e.g., 10% of each digit in MNIST).
    * **Best for:** **Mandatory for Classification tasks.** | `StratifiedKFold()` | `StratifiedShuffleSplit()` |
    
> HERE THE SHUFFLE SPLIT DO THE SAME AS THE KFOLD AND STRATIFIED, ONLY DIVIDES THE DATA
    
4. **ShuffleSplit:** Performs random and independent shuffles/draws for each round, without being tied to fixed blocks.
    * **Rule:** There may be overlap (a data point might be tested multiple times or not at all).
    * **Best for:** Very large datasets (Big Data). | `ShuffleSplit()` |

>KFold, StratifiedKFold, ShuffleSplit are division strategies. None of them train the model. They are just to create the "map" of how your dataset will be sliced.


## 2. Tuning Strategies (Search Methods)
Once you have defined your validation strategy (the "terrain"), you need to adjust the model's **Hyperparameters** (the "knobs").

### Parameters vs. Hyperparameters
* **Parameters:** Learned by the model during training (e.g., weights).
* **Hyperparameters:** Defined by **YOU** before training (e.g., `max_depth`, `n_estimators`).

### A. GridSearchCV (Exhaustive Search)
Tests **every single combination** of parameters in a provided grid.
* **Pros:** Guaranteed to find the best combination within the grid.
* **Cons:** Very slow and computationally expensive.
* **CV built-in:** It performs Cross-Validation for every combination automatically.

`GridSearchCV`

### B. RandomizedSearchCV (Random Search)
Selects random combinations from a distribution or list.
* **Pros:** Much faster than GridSearch. Usually finds an optimal solution in a fraction of the time.
* **Best for:** When you have many hyperparameters to test or a large dataset.

`RandomizedSearchCV`

## 3. Diagnosing Underfitting & Overfitting
When performing Tuning (Grid or Randomized Search), it is crucial to activate the 'return_train_score=True' parameter. This allows us to compare training performance against validation, revealing the real health of the model.

| Training Score | Validation Score | Diagnosis | Action |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| **Low** | **Low** | **Underfitting** | Increase model complexity / Better Features |
| **High** | **Low** | **Overfitting** | Regularization / More Data / Feature Selection |
| **High** | **High** | **Sweet Spot** | You found the best model! |

> **Pro-tip:** If your 'mean_train_score' is 0.99 and your 'mean_test_score' is 0.70, your model has "decorated" the workout (Overfitting). If both are 0.55, he hasn't even started learning (Underfitting).

## 4. Learning Curves & Bias-Variance Trade-off
The learning curve is the visual tool that **proves** whether the model is suffering from a lack of capacity or overconfidence.

`learning_curve()`

### A. Bias (Bias) - The Simplicity Error
Bias is linked to **Underfitting**. It occurs when the model makes very simple assumptions about the data.
* **Example:** Trying to use a straight line to explain data that makes a complex curve.
* **Symptom:** High error in both training and validation. The model is "biased" (it thinks it knows the pattern before it even sees the data).

### B. Variance (Variance) - The Sensitivity Error
Variance is linked to **Overfitting**. It occurs when the model is extremely sensitive to small variations in training data.
* **Example:** The model even learns the "noise" and random training failures.
* **Symptom:** Almost zero training error, but very high validation (test) error. He doesn't know how to deal with what he's never seen.

### C. The Trade-off (The Balance)
"Trade-off" is the central challenge of Machine Learning. it's an exchange, to gain something at one end, the other end must decrease (like a seesaw)

1. If you **decrease Bias** (make the model more powerful), the **Variance tends to increase**.
2. If you **decrease Variance** (make the model more rigid/simple), the **Bias tends to increase**.

**The goal of Tuning is to find the sweet spot where the sum of Bias + Variance is as small as possible.**

* **Underfitting:** High Bias / Low Variance.
* **Overfitting:** Low Bias / High Variance.
* **Sweet Spot:** The perfect balance for generalization.
