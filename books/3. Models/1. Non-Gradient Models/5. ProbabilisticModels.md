# PROBABILISTIC-BASED-MODELS

## Non-Gradient Core Concept: Probability & Statistics

**Pure probabilistic models like Naive Bayes do NOT use Gradient Descent.**

They are **analytical models** that:
- Calculate frequencies and means directly from the data.
- Use **Bayes' Theorem** to find the most likely class.
- Rely on **statistical distributions** (like the Gaussian bell curve).
- Do not update weights iteratively; they "count" and "divide".

---

# 1. Naive Bayes

## 1.1 What Is Naive Bayes?
Naive Bayes is a classification algorithm based on probabilities. It answers the question: *"Given feature X, what is the probability of this belonging to Class Y?"*

It is called **"Naive"** for one specific reason:
- It assumes that all features are **independent** of each other.
- *Example:* To the model, the "Red Color" of a fruit has zero relationship with its "Round Shape". In real life, we know they are related, but the algorithm ignores this to gain **extreme speed**.



### The Logic of Likelihood
Imagine you want to know if an email is **SPAM** because it contains the word "FREE":
1. The model looks at how many past SPAM emails had the word "FREE".
2. It looks at the overall probability of any email being SPAM.
3. It combines these to give a final probability score.

---

## 1.2 The "Flavors" of Naive Bayes (Probabilistic Kernels)

Unlike trees that change depth, Naive Bayes changes based on the **type of data** you have:

* **Gaussian Naive Bayes:** Used for **continuous numbers** (e.g., Height, Weight, Temperature).
    - It assumes data follows a **Normal Distribution** (the bell curve).
    

[Image of Gaussian distribution bell curve]


* **Multinomial Naive Bayes:** The king of **Text Classification**.
    - It works with **word counts/frequencies**. It focuses on how many times a word appears.

* **Bernoulli Naive Bayes:** Used for **binary** data (Yes/No, 0/1).
    - It only cares if a feature *exists* or not, ignoring the frequency.

---

## 1.3 How Naive Bayes Works (Workflow)

### Step-by-Step Process

1.  **Calculate Prior Probabilities:**
    - The model calculates the base probability of each class (e.g., 20% of all emails are SPAM).

2.  **Create a Likelihood Table:**
    - For every feature, the model counts how often it appears within each class.

3.  **Apply Bayes' Theorem:**
    - When a new data point arrives, the model multiplies the probabilities of all observed features.

4.  **Pick the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP):**
    - The class with the highest final probability wins the prediction.

---

## 1.4 Key Characteristics of Naive Bayes

- **Extremely Fast:** No training iterations; just direct calculations.
- **Works with small datasets:** It doesn't need millions of rows to understand basic statistics.
- **Robust to irrelevant features:** If a column doesn't help, its probability spreads out and doesn't affect the result much.
- **The "Zero Probability" Problem:** If it sees a word in "Test" that never appeared in "Train", the whole math becomes zero. (*Fixed with Laplace Smoothing*).



## 2. Key Takeaway

- **Probabilistic models are the "fastest" in ML.**
- They don't "hunt" for a line; they **describe the data mathematically**.
- They are the best starting point for **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** and problems where you need a "confidence score" rather than just a label.
